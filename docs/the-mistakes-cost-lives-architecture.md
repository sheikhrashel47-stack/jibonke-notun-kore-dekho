# THE MISTAKES THAT COST US OUR LIVES — Architecture

## Book identity

**Title:** THE MISTAKES THAT COST US OUR LIVES  
**বাংলা subtitle:** মানুষের করা সাধারণ ভুল, যেগুলো ধীরে ধীরে তার জীবনকে পিছিয়ে দেয়  
**Theme line:** সিদ্ধান্ত • অভ্যাস • অর্থ • সম্পর্ক • ক্যারিয়ার • মন • সময়  
**Format:** Bengali non-fiction / psychology / self-development / life strategy  
**Target reader:** 18–45-year-old general readers who feel stuck, distracted, financially stressed, uncertain, or repeatedly caught in the same patterns.

The book is designed as **12 parts × 10 chapters = 120 chapters**. The 120-chapter map is not filler; each chapter uses a distinct mistake, mechanism, consequence, correction, self-check, and action. Chapters are written in the sequence **NOTICE → NAME → UNDERSTAND → MEASURE → REPLACE → REVIEW**.

## Four-level analysis model

Every major chapter follows four layers: **The Behaviour**—what the person does; **The Psychology**—why the pattern is attractive or protective; **The Consequence**—short-term and long-term cost; and **The Correction**—a realistic system rather than a motivational slogan. The full chapter template adds context, fictionalized composite case or verified research case, turning point, alternative decision, lesson, self-check, and action plan.

## Case and evidence policy

Public events, companies, experiments, and named people are used only with reliable sources. Illustrative stories are labelled **“নিচের ঘটনাটি একটি fictionalized composite example।”** Ordinary students, employees, parents, teachers, freelancers, small-business owners, professionals, young adults, and older adults are represented. No living person is diagnosed, shamed, or used as a fictional personal story without clear labelling.

## Required special sections

### 100 Questions to Find Your Own Mistakes

One hundred unique questions covering time, attention, health, money, work, relationships, ego, fear, opportunity cost, regret, and identity. The questions must be diagnostic without pretending to be a clinical assessment.

### The 30-Day Error Reset

Day 1 Life Audit; Day 2 Time Audit; Day 3 Digital Audit; Day 4 Money Audit; Day 5 Sleep; Day 6 unfinished tasks; Day 7 values; Day 8 calendar; Day 9 attention; Day 10 environment; Day 11 spending triggers; Day 12 debt/obligations; Day 13 skill gap; Day 14 feedback; Day 15 boundaries; Day 16 difficult conversation; Day 17 decision journal; Day 18 error log; Day 19 health; Day 20 relationships; Day 21 one-goal experiment; Day 22 friction reduction; Day 23 replacement habit; Day 24 accountability; Day 25 risk map; Day 26 opportunity cost; Day 27 review; Day 28 course correction; Day 29 personal operating system; Day 30 commitment and next review date. Each day contains task, reflection, and action.

### The Cost of Delay

This section uses conceptual examples and explicitly labelled assumptions. A simple compound-growth illustration may be shown as `future value = present value × (1 + rate)^period`, without turning any example into investment advice. Sleep, skill-building, difficult conversations, debt, procrastination, and unhealthy relationships are analysed as accumulated costs, not deterministic predictions.

### 50 Expensive Life Mistakes

A quick-reference table where each entry has mistake, warning sign, consequence, and correction. The entries are not presented as a moral ranking; they are practical prompts.

### Mistake → Pattern → System

The core framework: identify the repeated event, understand its trigger and reward, replace the behaviour, measure the result, review the environment, and redesign the system.

### The Life Error Manual

A 20–30-page quick-reference section organised into daily, weekly, career, money, relationship, decision, mind, and success mistakes. Each entry uses **WARNING SIGN + CORRECTION**.

## Editorial boundaries

The book rejects toxic positivity, fake secrets, victim-blaming, deterministic psychology, and guaranteed success claims. It recognises luck, privilege, health, timing, family, structural limits, and opportunity differences while preserving responsibility for controllable factors. It does not provide personalised financial, medical, legal, or mental-health diagnosis or product recommendations.
