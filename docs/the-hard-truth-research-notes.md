# THE HARD TRUTH — Research Notes

This file records source anchors for the manuscript. Claims are intentionally phrased cautiously and are not presented as medical, legal, or individualized financial advice.

## Core evidence anchors

1. **Stress and decision-making:** Sarmiento et al. (2024), *Decision-making under stress: A psychological and neurobiological integrative model*, Brain, Behavior, & Immunity—Health. The review emphasizes that stress effects vary with stressor type, timing, context, individual differences, and task; stress can alter attention, memory, flexibility, and decision strategies. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11061251/

2. **Habit formation:** Singh et al. (2024), *Time to Form a Habit: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Health Behaviour Habit Formation and Its Determinants*, Healthcare. The review reports substantial individual variability and rejects the popular fixed “21 days” rule; stable context, repetition, timing, behavior type, and individual factors matter. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/

3. **Grief:** American Psychological Association, *Speaking of Psychology: How grieving changes the brain*, featuring Mary-Frances O’Connor. The source distinguishes grief from grieving, describes grief as a non-linear learning and adjustment process, and cautions against treating stage models as prescriptions. https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/grieving-changes-brain

4. **Emergency savings:** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, *An essential guide to building an emergency fund*. The guide defines an emergency fund as a cash reserve for unplanned expenses, notes that even small savings may help, and discusses goals, cash-flow monitoring, automatic contributions, accessibility, and consistent use rules. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/

5. **Sleep and mental health:** Scott et al. (2021), *Improving sleep quality leads to better mental health*, PMC. The study supports treating sleep as part of a balanced health system rather than glorifying sleep deprivation. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8651630/

6. **Cognitive bias:** Korteling et al. (2018), *A Neural Network Framework for Cognitive Bias*, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience / PMC. Use to explain heuristics and bias without claiming that people can remove all bias through willpower alone. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6129743/

7. **Decision-making and cognitive functions:** Müller et al. (2021), *Decision-making, cognitive functions, impulsivity, and media use*, PMC. Use for cautious discussion of working memory, executive functions, impulsivity, and decision context. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8547206/

## Editorial guardrails

The book will not promise that hard work guarantees success, that everyone can become wealthy, that failure is always beneficial, or that painful events happen for a reason. It will distinguish structural barriers from personal agency, explanation from excuse, persistence from sunk-cost attachment, and ambition from status comparison. The money chapter will use general educational information only and will avoid individualized investment recommendations.
